source: General endurance-training principles (Daniels, Pfitzinger/Douglas, ACSM exercise guidelines), summarised in my own words

## Easy runs and the talk test
An easy run should feel conversational: you can speak in full sentences. If you can only say a few words, you are going too fast. Most weekly running (around 80%) should be easy so that the harder sessions and the long run can be done well and recovered from.

## Why easy pace is not race pace
Running easy runs at goal race pace turns every run into a medium-hard run, which builds fatigue without extra fitness. Easy pace is normally noticeably slower than half-marathon race pace, especially for someone whose current fitness is below the goal pace.

## Effort scale (RPE 1-10) without a heart-rate monitor
RPE 1-2 is walking pace. RPE 3-4 is easy and conversational. RPE 5-6 is steady and comfortably hard. RPE 6-7 is tempo and half-marathon race effort. RPE 8-9 is hard, only a few words possible. RPE 10 is an all-out sprint. Without heart rate, use RPE with the talk test, pace and how the run felt.

## Riegel race-time projection
A common way to project one race distance from another is time2 = time1 x (distance2 / distance1) ^ 1.06. It assumes similar training for both distances. It tends to be optimistic when weekly mileage and long-run experience are low, so projections from short runs (such as 5K) overstate half-marathon ability. Recent runs of 8-12 km give more trustworthy projections.

## Pace on hot, hilly or windy days
Pace is a poor guide in heat, hills or wind. Run by effort and the talk test and expect pace to be slower, often by 10-20 seconds per km or more in hot, humid weather. Judging a run by pace on such days causes pushing too hard.

## Phone GPS pace is noisy
Phone GPS can wobble, especially near buildings or trees. Average pace for the whole run is more reliable than instantaneous pace or single-kilometre splits. Treat small differences between runs as noise.

## Strides
Strides are 15-20 second relaxed accelerations with full recovery, done after an easy run a couple of times a week. They improve running form and leg speed without much fatigue. They are optional and are not all-out sprints.

## Tempo and steady running
Steady or tempo running is comfortably hard (RPE 6-7), a pace you could hold for about an hour in a well-trained runner. Short blocks, for example 2 x 1.5 km with a jog between, are a gentle way to introduce it. Keep them modest and only when recovered.

## Race-pace familiarisation
Running a few short repeats at goal pace lets you learn what the target feels like. If goal pace is much faster than your current easy pace, keep the repeats short and stop early if form or breathing falls apart. They do not prove you can hold that pace for 21 km.
