## Fuelling during long runs
For runs longer than about 75 minutes, take around 30-60 g of carbohydrate per hour, starting in the first 30-45 minutes rather than waiting until tired. Gels, chews, sports drink or banana pieces all work. Sip water with gels. Practise fuelling in long runs so your stomach adapts.

## How many gels for a half marathon
A finish around 2.5 hours at 30-60 g/hour needs roughly 75-150 g of carbohydrate, which is about 3-6 gels of 25 g depending on the product. Begin around 30-40 minutes in and repeat every 30-40 minutes. Practise the exact product and timing.

## Fluid during runs
Drink to thirst and aim to avoid big fluid losses, roughly 400-800 ml per hour depending on heat and sweat rate. Drinking much more than you lose can cause low blood sodium (hyponatraemia), which is dangerous. Weigh before and after a run to learn your sweat rate: each kg lost is about 1 litre.

## Electrolytes and sodium
Sodium matters when sweating a lot, in hot weather or on runs over about an hour. Typical guidance is about 300-700 mg sodium per hour for heavy or salty sweaters, from sports drink, tablets or salty food. Short cool runs do not need electrolyte products.

## Daily hydration
Drink regularly through the day and aim for pale-yellow urine. A rough daily baseline is about 30-35 ml per kg of body weight from all drinks and food, plus extra for training and hot weather. Drink 400-600 ml in the 2-3 hours before a long run, and rehydrate after with fluid and some salt.

## Carbohydrate the day before the race
For a half marathon, a carb-up is helpful but modest: raise carbohydrate to about 6-8 g/kg the day before while reducing fibre and fat, and eat familiar foods. You do not need to over-stuff. Expect weight to rise 1-2 kg from stored glycogen and water; that is normal and useful.

## Race-morning breakfast
Eat about 1-2 g/kg of carbohydrate roughly 2-3 hours before the start, low in fibre and fat, for example white toast or a bagel with honey and a banana. For an early start, eat slightly less or earlier if you cannot stomach a large meal. A gel 15 minutes before the start tops up fuel.

## Early-morning running and fasted runs
Short easy runs can be done on a small snack or fasted, but long and quality runs perform better with some carbohydrate beforehand. Fasting for long runs raises fatigue and does not help fat loss over time. Do not skip fuelling to speed weight loss.

## Stomach problems while running
Common causes are too much fibre, fat or fluid before the run, new foods, very concentrated gels without water, and going out too fast. Reduce fibre the day before, test fuel in training, and take gels with water. Visit the toilet before the start.

## Heat and sweat
In hot or humid weather, sweat losses and sodium losses rise. Start hydrated, carry or plan fluids, and use electrolytes for runs over about an hour. Running early in the morning, as in the 5 AM race, is a real advantage.
