source: General race-preparation principles (Mujika & Padilla taper research summaries, Pfitzinger/Douglas, Daniels), summarised in my own words

## How to taper for a half marathon
Taper over about 1-2 weeks. Cut total volume by roughly 30-50% while keeping some short sessions at goal effort and keeping run frequency. The aim is to arrive fresh while holding fitness. Fitness is not gained in the last two weeks, only fatigue is lost.

## Do not add training late
Adding extra volume or harder sessions in the last two weeks to catch up reduces freshness and raises injury risk. If the plan has gaps, accept it and race to your current fitness.

## Taper restlessness
Feeling sluggish, restless or doubtful in taper is normal. Keep routines, sleep well, and resist extra runs. Short easy runs with a few strides keep legs feeling sharp.

## Race week rules
Nothing new: no new shoes, kit, food, supplements or gels. Wear what you have trained in. Prepare the kit, bib and transport the day before. Keep hydration normal and avoid heavy alcohol.

## Race-morning timeline for a 5 AM start
Wake about 2.5 hours before the start. Eat a familiar low-fibre, low-fat carb breakfast about 2-2.5 hours before. Sip fluid with electrolytes. Arrive early, do 10 minutes of easy jogging and mobility with a few strides, and take a gel about 15 minutes before the start. Rehearse this timeline on a long run so it is familiar.

## Pacing the half marathon
Even pacing wins. Start slightly slower than goal pace for the first 2-3 km, settle into target effort (RPE 6-7), and only push in the final 3-5 km if you feel good. Going out too fast is the most common reason for slowing badly in the second half.

## What a 2:30 half marathon requires
Finishing in 2:30 needs an average of about 7:06.6 per km, which is 35:33 per 5 km. A rough sanity check is whether recent training runs show you can run 10 km at or near that pace. If current evidence shows you are slower, choose a realistic primary goal and treat 2:30 as an upside, not a promise.

## If you are behind goal pace in the race
Do not surge to catch up. Slow, steady corrections cost less than a big surge that causes a late collapse. If the day is hot or you feel unwell, drop pace and finish healthy.

## Race fuelling and aid stations
Take 30-60 g of carbohydrate per hour, starting around 30-40 minutes in, for example a gel about every 35-40 minutes with water. Drink at aid stations and slow or walk through them if needed rather than skipping fluids. Do not take more fluid than thirst demands.

## After the race
Keep walking, drink with a little salt, and eat carbohydrate plus about 25-30 g protein within an hour. Expect heavy legs for several days. Do easy movement only for the first week.
